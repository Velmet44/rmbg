FAQ

Is it really free? What's the catch?+

Yes. MIT-licensed, no account, no credits, no watermark, no artificial resolution cap. The ~98 MB AI model downloads once and lives in your browser cache.

Do my photos leave my device?+

No. Inference runs on-device; image bytes are never uploaded. The only download is the model itself.

Why is the first run slow?+

First visit downloads the model and builds the session; return visits skip the download. GPU-backed browsers are fastest with a slower CPU fallback — the app tells you which it's using.

Erase, restore, guided, recompute — which do I use?+

Brushes for small slips (paint, then Apply commits); guided click for one-tap coherent regions; drag-a-box recompute when the AI misread a whole area — the rest of the mask stays untouched. Everything is undoable.

Which files go in and out?+

In: PNG, JPG, WebP, AVIF, BMP, GIF (first frame); HEIC isn't supported yet. Out: PNG, WebP, JPEG, plus AVIF where your browser can encode it.

Why does JPEG demand a background?+

JPEG can't store transparency, so you choose the background explicitly — nothing is ever flattened silently.

How good are hair and tricky edges?+

Float alpha masks preserve hair and semi-transparency, and originals are never mutated. Zoom to 100% to inspect, feather and defringe to finish.

How does batch work?+

Up to 24 images in a sequential on-device queue with per-item retry. Open any item in the full editor, export all as one ZIP.

It failed — now what?+

Model won't start: retry, it's usually a download or GPU hiccup. Out of memory: use a smaller image and close tabs. Undecodable file: convert it first. Still stuck: use Report an issue in the footer — diagnostics attach automatically, no pixels included.

What model runs, and under what license?+

BiRefNet-lite 512px, MIT — provenance pinned in models/manifest.json. Heavier non-commercial weights are bring-your-own only, never bundled.
